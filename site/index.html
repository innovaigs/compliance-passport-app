01

The problem

The same answers, retyped forever.

Buyers push supplier risk, ESG, labour and security questionnaires through Coupa Risk Assess, Avetta, EcoVadis, Sedex, Ariba and their own bespoke portals. The answers already exist inside the supplier's own policy library. A human still spends days finding them, rewriting them into whatever grid that buyer sent, and hand-typing the document references.

The wasted hours are the visible cost. The quieter one is drift. What you told one buyer about your maximum working week is not quite what you told the next. The subcontracting answer you gave in March was written by someone who has since left. Each questionnaire is answered from memory, from a stale copy of last year's file, or from whichever version of the policy happened to be open.

The same question, asked by two buyers State the maximum number of working hours permitted per week, including overtime.

SUBMITTED · BUYER A14 MAR 2026

“A maximum of 48 hours per week including overtime.”

Answered by the operations lead, from the policy draft.

SUBMITTED · BUYER B02 JUL 2026

“Up to 60 hours during peak demand periods, by agreement.”

Answered by a colleague, from a scheduling spreadsheet.

Contradiction

Both submissions are on file with enterprise buyers. Neither author saw the other. Nothing in the process compares them — and nothing will, until an auditor or a buyer does it for you.

There is no single place where every answer your company has ever submitted sits next to every other one, and no moment at which the contradiction surfaces. Inconsistency across submissions is not an administrative annoyance. It is an audit exposure that grows with every questionnaire you answer.

02

The insight

A questionnaire is not a form to fill in. It is a query against your evidence.

Treat it as a form and every buyer is a new project: read the grid, hunt through the policy folder, paraphrase, paste, chase the document reference, repeat. The work scales with the number of buyers, and none of it accumulates.

Treat it as a query and the shape of the problem inverts. The company's policies, controls, registers and records are structured once — not as files in a folder, but as a graph, resolved to the clause, where each node knows what it asserts, what document it lives in, when it was last reviewed and who owns it. Now a question is not a blank to be filled. It is a read: what in this graph speaks to maximum working hours, and how strongly?

The buyer's format stops mattering, because the format is only the envelope. Coupa's grid, Avetta's portal, an EcoVadis module and a procurement manager's own spreadsheet are four different renderings of overlapping questions about the same underlying facts. Once the facts are addressable, the rendering is a translation problem — and translation is the cheap part.

The asset was never the completed questionnaire. It was the structured evidence underneath it — and every company that answers by hand throws that asset away, one submission at a time.

03

How it works

Three steps, and only one of them is yours.

You structure your evidence once. Everything after that is a read against it.

Step 01

Build the evidence graph

Your policies, procedures, registers, training records and certificates are ingested once and structured to the clause — not summarised, not embedded and forgotten. Each clause keeps its identity: the document it belongs to, its section, its version, its review date, its owner. A working-hours limit in LAB-POL-02 §3.1 is a distinct, addressable fact, not a sentence buried in a PDF. This is the only step that costs you time, and you do it once.

Step 02

Run any questionnaire

Upload the buyer's file exactly as it arrived. The agent inspects the shape it has been sent — a merged-cell workbook, a locked template, a portal export, a form nobody has ever seen — and writes a parser for that specific file at request time. It maps each question onto the graph, drafts the answer from your own evidence, and attaches the precise document and clause behind every claim it makes. Nothing is asserted that does not trace back to a source.

Step 03

Review and return

Every draft carries a confidence score derived from evidence strength — how directly the clause answers the question, how current the document is, how much interpretation was required. Strong answers pass through. Weak ones are surfaced for a human, so review time goes where judgment is actually needed instead of being spread evenly across 200 questions. The finished set is written back into the buyer's original file, formatting intact, ready to return.

04

The refusal

The most valuable thing it produces is the answer it won't give.

A system that generates fluent compliance prose is a liability. When there is no evidence, Compliance Passport does not compose a plausible sentence. It files a gap. Here is what that looks like on a real question.

Question 41 of 53 · Coupa Risk Assess · Information security

Does your organisation hold a current ISO/IEC 27001 certification? Provide the certificate number, issuing body and expiry date.

Supplier does not hold ISO/IEC 27001.

What a generative tool returns

“Yes — we maintain a comprehensive information security management system aligned with ISO 27001 principles, including documented access control, incident response and asset management procedures.”

Fluent, reassuring, and an unsupported claim in a document the buyer will keep on file. Not returned.

Gap GAP-014 No answer submitted
Why no answer
No evidence in the library supports a certification claim. The closest related evidence is INFOSEC-POL-01 §4.2 (access control) and INFOSEC-POL-01 §6.1 (incident response) — internal policy, which is not certification and cannot be presented as such.
What honestly can be said
Not certified. Documented information security policy in force since 12 August 2026, owner named, review cycle annual. Offered to the reviewer as a truthful partial response, clearly labelled as policy rather than certification.
Document that closes it
ISO/IEC 27001 certificate issued by an accredited certification body, with the accompanying Statement of Applicability.
Also unblocks
  • Q42Provide the scope statement of your certification.
  • Q43Date of your most recent external surveillance audit.
  • Q47Is your information security programme independently audited by a third party?
  • Q51Attach evidence of certification for any subcontractor handling buyer data.

One document. Five questions, in this assessment alone — and in every assessment after it.

Every gap recorded this way accumulates into a gap register: an honest, continuously maintained list of what the company cannot yet claim, ranked by how many questions each missing artifact is blocking across every buyer who has ever asked.

This is not a tool that makes you look compliant. It is a tool that tells you what you can honestly claim today, and hands you the roadmap for the rest — generated for free, as a by-product of answering.

05

Architecture

The runtime is structural, not incidental.

Every buyer's file has a different shape, so no universal parser exists and none can be written in advance. The agent authors a parser at request time — which means model-written code is about to execute against confidential compliance documents. That cannot happen inside the application process.

A sandbox per run, not per tenant

Each run gets its own disposable environment, created for that request and destroyed when it completes. Model-authored code never touches the application process, and nothing it writes outlives the run that produced it.

Isolation as a consequence, not a feature

Because the sandbox exists only for one run, one supplier's policy library is never co-resident with another's. There is no shared process for a leak to cross.

Adaptation is the product

A fixed set of connectors would cover the portals and fail on the spreadsheet a procurement manager built last Tuesday. Writing the parser per request is what makes “any format” a real claim rather than a roadmap item.

The execution log is evidence too

Each run leaves a record of what was parsed, what was read from the graph, what was drafted and what was refused. That log is the beginning of a defensible audit trail, not a debugging artifact.

06

The platform

Answering is the entry point, not the product.

Once every answer, its evidence and its gaps live in one structured place, the questionnaire stops being the unit of work. The graph becomes the compliance record of the company — and it can be read forwards, backwards, and from the buyer's side.

Shipping

Answer any assessment from your own evidence, honestly.

  • Adaptive ingestion of any questionnaire formatA parser written per file, at request time — portal exports, locked templates, bespoke spreadsheets.

Next

In design

Make the answers accountable to each other, and to time.

  • Answer memoryEvery answer ever submitted, versioned by buyer and by date — one place where the company's own record lives.
  • Cross-buyer consistency detectionFlag where this run's answer contradicts what you told a previous buyer, before it is submitted rather than after it is discovered.
  • Evidence freshnessPolicies carry review dates. Warn when an answer now rests on a stale document, and identify every submitted answer affected.
  • Gap remediation trackingOwners and target dates on each gap. Closing one automatically unlocks the questions it was blocking.

Later

Direction

The graph as shared infrastructure between supplier and buyer.

  • Direct connectors to the assessment networksCoupa, Avetta, EcoVadis, Sedex and Ariba — ingest assessments by API instead of by file.
  • Continuous assuranceWhen a policy is revised, identify which previously submitted answers are now inaccurate and notify the affected buyers.
  • Multi-entity workspacesGroups, agencies and BPOs answering on behalf of several legal entities, each with its own evidence and its own truth.
  • The buyer sideProcurement teams grading incoming submissions and detecting unsupported claims — the same graph, read from the other end.
  • Full provenance exportEvery answer's chain of evidence, plus the sandbox execution log, as a defensible record for an external auditor.

Software vendors

Security review, subprocessors, resilience

There is already a category of tools that automate security questionnaires for software companies. They are built around the assumption that the respondent is a SaaS vendor answering SIG, CAIQ or a SOC 2 mapping.

That leaves out most of the companies actually being assessed. The labour, ethics, ESG and supplier risk assessments pushed at services suppliers — the staffing firm, the interpreter marketplace, the logistics operator, the facilities contractor — go through Coupa Risk Assess, Avetta, EcoVadis and Sedex, ask about working hours and subcontracting and grievance procedures rather than encryption at rest, and are answered by hand. That work is unserved.

I run a multilingual BPO and interpreter marketplace. We are a supplier to a large staffing enterprise.

In August 2026 we adopted a formal ethics and labour compliance programme — five policies, four registers, a training curriculum. Weeks of work, properly done, all of it written down.

Days later the buyer's supplier risk assessment arrived through Coupa. Fifty-three questions. Every single answer already existed in documents we had just finished writing. It still would have taken two days: find the clause, rewrite it to fit their grid, type the document reference, move to the next question, discover the next question is asking the same thing in a different way.

And this was the first buyer. The next one would send a different file and ask for the same facts in a different order, and we would do it all again from scratch.

That is the whole idea. The answers were an asset we were about to throw away.

Your compliance answers are an asset. Answer it once. Answer it everywhere.
